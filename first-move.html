The first time you step onto the course with intention, everything feels different.

You are no longer just there to play.

You are aware that something else is happening.

Most women don’t know what to do next.

You’re invited to play.

You don’t fully know the group.

You can feel that this round matters, but you can’t yet explain why.

This is the moment where most either:

Try too hard… or disappear completely.

Neither works.

The first move is not what you say.

It is what you observe.

Within the first 15 minutes, you should be watching:

Who speaks and who others listen to.
Who sets the tone without effort.
Who is being included, and who is not.

This tells you everything.

From there, your positioning becomes simple.

You don’t try to lead.

You don’t try to impress.

You align yourself near the center of influence.

Close enough to be included.

Far enough to observe.

Then you wait.

The first time you speak should do one thing:

Add direction.

Not energy.

Not noise.

Direction.

This is where most get it wrong.

They talk too early.

Or they never enter at all.

Both remove them from the real opportunity.

The follow-up is where it becomes real.

Not on the course.

After.

Short. Direct. Relevant to the moment that actually mattered.
