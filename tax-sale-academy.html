Did You Know?

You can purchase properties for as little as $500 at tax sales. Some go even lower. This is one of the most accessible real estate entry points available to everyday investors.

Set Your Goal First

Before you bid on anything, get clear on your WHY. Are you building generational wealth through land banking? Seeking rental income? Appreciation? Your goal shapes every decision you make.

This is not a get-rich-quick scheme. But you WILL get rich if you have clear goals backed by consistent action.

What Is a Tax Deed?

A legal document granting property ownership to a buyer when the original owner fails to pay taxes. The government sells the property at public auction to recover the unpaid amount. Issued after years of nonpayment as a last resort for municipalities.

What Is a Tax Lien?

A claim the government places on a property when the owner fails to pay taxes. Investors purchase lien certificates at auction, paying the owed taxes on the homeowner's behalf. The homeowner must repay the investor with interest. If they do not, the investor can foreclose.

The Key Difference

In a tax deed state, you purchase the property outright. In a tax lien state, you purchase the certificate and earn interest while waiting for repayment. Florida operates as both, giving you two powerful strategies in your own backyard.

PROS

  • Less competition
  • Secured by the property
  • Entry as low as $500

The Process

Step-by-step from zero to land owner.

1

Set Your Investment Goals

Decide whether you want long-term appreciation, rental income, or a quick flip. Write your goals down before taking any other step. This shapes your entire strategy.

2

Research the Market

Use Zillow, Realtor.com, and Redfin to compare prices and trends. Access county assessor websites for ownership history and tax data. Use Google Earth and GIS tools to assess topography, zoning, and flood zones. Attend your local REIA for networking and insider knowledge you cannot find in data alone.

3

Locate the County Tax Office

Find the tax office for the county where you want to invest. Ask when the next tax sale is and whether it is a deed or lien sale. For lien sales, ask about the redemption period (typically one year in most counties).

When you buy from the county: your money is government-backed, returns can be very high, and the property cannot be sold without paying you first.
4

Register as a Bidder

Every county requires registration before you can participate. Register on the county's website. You can register in multiple counties at the same time to maximize your reach.

5

Know the Difference: Lien vs. Deed

Tax Lien: County sells the certificate to you. Owner repays you with interest. You return the certificate and collect your principal plus interest. Investors get paid.

Tax Deed: Government auctions the property to the highest bidder. The sale is final. Some deeds carry a Right of Redemption window, during which the owner can reclaim by paying delinquent taxes and penalties. Once that period ends, you foreclose it out and own the property free and clear.

6

Scrub the Property List

Once you receive the auction list, research only the properties that meet your criteria. Check value, neighborhood trends, total tax liability, additional liens, zoning, and condition. Drive by whenever possible.

7

Attend and Bid

Arrive early. Stick to your maximum bid. Keep track of every property you bid on. Have your funds ready since payment is often required within 24 to 48 hours of winning.

8

Wait for the Redemption Period

If the owner does not redeem the property, you receive the deed. If they do redeem it, you receive a check from the county for your principal plus interest. Either outcome, you win.

9

Consider Over-the-Counter (OTC)

You do not have to wait for a tax sale. Properties that did not sell at auction go onto the county's over-the-counter list. Buy directly from the county any time, with no competitive bidding, often at an even lower price.

Call your county and ask for their over-the-counter list. Use a spreadsheet to stay organized as you research each property.
10

Fund Your Purchases

Cash, cashier's checks, and wire transfers are standard. Some counties accept credit cards. Using 0% APR cards can be a powerful strategy if you have a clear payoff plan. Always confirm accepted payment methods with the county before auction day.

11

Build Your Legal Team

Never buy land without a real estate attorney. You also need a bookkeeper or CPA. Real estate investing is a business. Treat it accordingly. Your local REIA is the best place to find qualified professionals.

12

Reinvest and Repeat

Reinvest your returns into more properties. Compound your wealth over time. Rinse and repeat.
